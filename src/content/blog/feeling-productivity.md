Sometimes all it takes to find your legs in a new place is to <span class="blog-accent-warn">identify your strengths</span>, find a way to contribute, and <span class="blog-accent-warn">lead with those strengths</span>. Show your capabilities and reach for even small wins to open up new opportunities and challenges. Imposter syndrome fades as self-actualization finds a way. <span class="blog-accent-warn">"Show me something you can do"</span> turns into <span class="blog-accent-info">"What else can you do?"</span>.

In the last week I was able to leverage my knowledge in audio editing and synthesis to get my first felt win on a new project. Now to address the **<span class="blog-accent-info">#ai</span>** tag on this post and answer some of the questions raised in my last post about how to use ai in your own way.

The project called for synthesized speech. <span class="blog-accent-warn">Drawing on prior work</span>, I identified a speech synthesis approach that fit the project's constraints, then used generative AI to make it practical: Claude helped me build a harness and lightweight GUI so the model was easy to operate for our needs. <span class="blog-accent-info">AI tools are enablers</span>. They accelerate <span class="blog-accent-info">creative problem solving</span> and reward <span class="blog-accent-info">breadth of knowledge</span>.
## <span class="blog-accent-info">Foremost I am a UX & Product designer.</span>
I also have coding and product management experience. In a <span class="blog-accent-warn">"show me something you can do"</span> environment, that breadth pulls me in many directions at once, and it becomes <span class="blog-accent-warn">difficult to find a win.</span> This project ran on early childhood development, a field I have no background in. I could not tell a <span class="blog-accent-info">productive direction</span> from a <span class="blog-accent-warn">dead end</span>.

Without a domain expert, I would have spent significant time on foundational questions the field has already answered. MindsEmerge founder Kristy Stark made that unnecessary. In a single working session, she redirected my research plan from surveying established knowledge toward the <span class="blog-accent-info">customer discovery</span> questions our survey needed. She knew what was already known, my job became finding what wasn't.
